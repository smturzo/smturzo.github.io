bargeen turzo

Principal Research Scientist, GSK. Protein design, quantum chemistry, and machine learning.

Brooklyn, New York

I’m a computational scientist working where quantum chemistry, molecular modeling, and machine learning meet. My work is about making computational protein design accurate enough to trust: pairing physics-based scoring with modern foundation models, and holding the results to experimental validation.

I’m currently a Principal Research Scientist at GSK, where I benchmark foundation models for antibody design and lead development of a new model architecture built specifically for antibody discovery. Before that I spent three years as a Research Fellow at the Flatiron Institute (Simons Foundation), where I co-developed AgentRosetta — an LLM agent that drives Rosetta for physics-based protein design — and engineered core parts of the open-source Masala software suite.

I did my PhD in Chemistry at Ohio State University with Steffen Lindert, developing methods that fold sparse experimental data — particularly ion-mobility mass spectrometry — into protein structure prediction. That work produced PARCS and the ROSIE-PARCS webserver, and showed that a cheap, low-resolution shape measurement can meaningfully improve structure prediction.

I also review for Nature Communications, Angewandte Chemie International Edition, Computational Biology and Chemistry, and PLOS ONE.

selected publications

  1. ICML
    Protein Design with AgentRosetta: A Case Study for Specialized Scientific Agents
    Jacopo Teneggi, S. M. Bargeen Alam Turzo, Tanya Marwah, and 4 more authors
    In International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), 2026
    Abs Bib

    AgentRosetta is an LLM-based scientific agent integrated with the Rosetta modeling suite for physics-based protein design. Structured agent-environment interfaces enable iterative refinement of canonical and non-canonical protein designs, and we show that environment design is critical for reliable LLM control of complex scientific software.

  2. Ion Mobility Mass Spectrometry Guided Modeling with AlphaFold and Rosetta Improves Protein Complex Structure Prediction
    Aravind Narayanasamy, Zachary C. Drake, S. M. Bargeen Alam Turzo, and 4 more authors
    Under peer review, 2026
    Abs Bib

    A hybrid modeling framework integrating ion mobility mass spectrometry collision cross section data with AlphaFold and Rosetta. A Rosetta docking pipeline guided by experimental CCS constraints and a new scoring function improved RMSD for 68% (26/38) of benchmark complexes, including corrections from >4 A to <4 A.

  3. bioRxiv
    The open-source Masala software suite: Facilitating rapid methods development for synthetic heteropolymer design
    Tristan Zaborniak, Nasim Azadvari, Qiyao Zhu, and 4 more authors
    bioRxiv, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Masala is a modular, free, and open-source set of C++ libraries designed to extend or replace Rosetta with a modern architecture optimized for current computing hardware. It automates API layer creation and implements efficient real-valued local and cost function network optimizers, achieving up to 100x speed improvements in tasks like hydrogen bond network evaluation and buried polar atom checks.

  4. Nat. Commun.
    Protein shape sampled by ion mobility mass spectrometry consistently improves protein structure prediction
    S. M. Bargeen Alam Turzo, Justin T. Seffernick, Amber D. Rolland, and 5 more authors
    Nature Communications, 2022
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    PARCS is a rapid and accurate method in Rosetta to compute collision cross sections from protein structures, matching the accuracy and improving on the speed of existing tools. Ion mobility data significantly enhances computational protein structure prediction accuracy, and an IM-based confidence score distinguishes accurate from inaccurate predictions in the absence of a known native structure.
